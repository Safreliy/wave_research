Dear Editor,

Please consider my manuscript, "Geometry-consistent conservative transfer of
potential-flow states to an embedded-boundary volume-of-fluid solver," for
publication as an original research article in Computational Mathematics and
Mathematical Physics.

The paper presents a conservative transfer construction for potential-flow
boundary-integral states and a two-phase embedded-boundary VOF receiver.
Its main development is a consistent boundary-normal extension: the physical
boundary and its neighbouring Cartesian edge use the same prescribed trace
in moment integration. A local estimate shows cancellation of the trace-error
term otherwise amplified by inverse liquid-strip thickness. Identical shared
edge integrals retain an algebraic liquid-moment closure identity.

The construction reduces global, liquid-volume-weighted velocity error in
all 21 analytic initial-field tests against a practical bilinear control
using the same streamfunction, by factors 2.71–32.07. An isolated higher-order
physical trace is retained as an ablation and does not achieve this result.
Native wave continuations on two grids, including a time-step halving, show
7.88–10.25-fold reductions in propagated velocity error and 65.0–82.9-fold
reductions in surface-profile error. These norms use exact-initial-input
numerical trajectories at identical receiver settings and were independently
recomputed from archived raw fields.
An additional small-amplitude two-fluid wave test uses an independent
analytic linear reference, with a rest control and amplitude halving.
A complete factorial comparison separates the boundary extension from a
density-weighted mixture assignment. Together they reduce physical
surface-profile error by 52.19% relative to the original practical
assignment at the endpoint; at common mixture assignment, the extension
contributes a 5.49% reduction. The first intermediate time favours the
practical control within mixture assignment and is reported explicitly.
These physical errors are distinguished from the larger transfer-only gains.

For a fixed overturning-wave state, matching the receiver geometry removes
a measured loading loss. The original fitted moments also reduce pooled
error on 12 selected bed-cut cells by 7.27 times against an independent BIE
reference, with stricter quadrature confirming that comparison.

The new extension and its estimate apply to flat surface strips in a
two-dimensional periodic setting. The manuscript distinguishes propagated
transfer error from the receiver's physical truncation error and retains
the baseline failures that motivate the construction. Frozen source,
declared comparison matrices, raw-field audits and input hashes document
the new experiments; older source-provenance limitations remain explicit.

The code and selected experimental evidence are available at
https://github.com/Safreliy/wave_research/tree/main/research/releases/transfer_20261001
