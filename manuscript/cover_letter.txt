Dear Editor,

Please consider my manuscript, "Geometry-consistent conservative transfer of
potential-flow states to an embedded-boundary volume-of-fluid solver," for
publication as an original research article in Computational Mathematics and
Mathematical Physics under the traditional publication model.

The paper develops a conservative transfer from potential-flow boundary
states to liquid-cell velocity means in an embedded-boundary volume-of-fluid
solver. Its central contribution is a matched boundary-normal extension for
thin strips beneath a flat free surface. The physical boundary and the
neighbouring Cartesian edge share one trace, removing a specific source of
error amplification. A local estimate covers the implemented blended
operator and states sufficient conditions for second-order accuracy.

Analytic tests isolate this mechanism from higher-order interpolation of the
physical trace alone. Three-grid initial refinement gives observed orders
2.005 and 2.137. Subsequent calculations on one documented receiver build
show smaller propagated transfer errors at every recorded nonzero time on
all three grids. An independent linear two-fluid wave comparison reduces
endpoint physical profile error by 5.49% and 2.81% on two grids with receiver
velocity assignment held fixed. The manuscript reports the intermediate
coarse-grid comparison favouring the control and distinguishes these
physical errors from discrepancies against exact-input numerical trajectories.

Code, input and output fields, source and build records, and audit-replay
instructions are archived at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23111007.
The archived manuscript is an openly available research version; this work
has not been published in a journal. This is its first journal submission,
and it is not under consideration elsewhere.
