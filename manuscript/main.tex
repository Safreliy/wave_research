\title{Conservative divergence-conforming state transfer from adaptive
potential-flow BIE to embedded VOF:\\overturning waves over variable bathymetry}
\author{Saveliy Baturin}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
A structure-preserving one-way state transfer is developed from an adaptive
fully nonlinear Euler boundary-integral equation (BIE) solver to an embedded
two-phase volume-of-fluid (VOF) receiver.  The contribution is not BIE--VOF
hybridisation itself, but a handoff that matches the receiver's discrete
invariants.  Integrated panel circulations preserve the adaptive BIE state;
shared face-averaged BIE fluxes are fitted by a vertex streamfunction so that
the MAC face field is divergence-free by construction; receiver momentum is
included as a quadrature constraint; and the full-cell liquid aperture
$q=f c_s$ is transported with a shared embedded-boundary flux.  The BIE also
uses a nonlocal-gap trigger for oversampled close evaluation.  After the
single transfer, only the VOF solver advances the solution.

Verification separates operator accuracy, conservation and interface
geometry.  On a manufactured folded boundary, oversampling reduces the
relative Dirichlet-to-Neumann error from 6.36 to $6.91\times10^{-2}$ at 64
panels despite similarly small linear residuals.  A solitary-wave translation
has shape and speed errors $1.82\times10^{-5}$ and
$8.40\times10^{-5}$.  Manufactured aperture transport is first-order on the
three finest grids and preserves liquid volume and $0\le q\le c_s$ to
roundoff.  Across the three production receiver grids, the constrained
handoff has at most 1.56\% face-flux error, 2.78\% surface-velocity error and
$7.09\times10^{-9}$ relative receiver-momentum error.

An overturning-wave state over variable bathymetry is continued to
nondimensional time 6 on three nested
receiver grids.  Maximum relative liquid-volume drift is
$8.56\times10^{-8}$, $6.43\times10^{-8}$ and $5.67\times10^{-9}$ on levels
8--10, with no aperture-bound violation.  Level 10 produces a transient
disconnected gas component at $t=1.96$, but the event is absent at the two
coarser levels and its detected onset varies by 0.06 under conservative raster
coarsening.  At $t=6$, the level-9/10 upper-profile and crest differences are
8.18\% and 6.25\%.  The method therefore achieves stable conservative
continuation and exposes a resolution-dependent topology transition, but the
present calculation does not establish grid-converged wave impact or air
entrainment.  The verified result is therefore a conservative,
divergence-conforming model transition rather than a converged prediction of
breaking topology.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} conservative state transfer; divergence-conforming
transfer; boundary integral method; volume-of-fluid method; embedded
boundaries; adaptive mesh refinement; free-surface topology change

\section{Introduction}

Boundary-integral descriptions of inviscid water waves resolve vertical and
overturning free surfaces without a height-function restriction
\cite{LonguetHigginsCokelet1976,DoldPeregrine1986,BakerMeironOrszag1982}.
They remain efficient while the liquid is irrotational and the interface is a
single non-self-intersecting curve, but cease to describe entrained air,
vorticity production and topology change after impact.  Fully nonlinear BEM
calculations have reproduced solitary-wave shoaling and classified breaking
regimes over beaches and obstacles \cite{GrilliSvendsenSubramanya1997}, while
two-phase VOF methods naturally continue through interface reconnection.

Hybridisation itself is well established.  Lachaume et al. initialised a
Navier--Stokes/VOF breaking-wave calculation from a potential-flow BEM and
compared solitary-wave evolution with experiments \cite{LachaumeEtAl2003}.
Colicchio, Greco and Faltinsen coupled BEM and Navier--Stokes/level-set
subdomains for deforming and fragmented interfaces
\cite{ColicchioGrecoFaltinsen2006}.  Kim, Yamashiro and Yoshida developed a
two-way BEM--SMAC/VOF decomposition for long random-wave calculations
\cite{KimYamashiroYoshida2010}.  More recently, Khait and Ma combined fully
nonlinear quasi-potential and two-phase Navier--Stokes models for energetic
breaking waves \cite{KhaitMa2021}, while Kanehira et al. embedded a GPU-SPH
region in a three-dimensional fully nonlinear potential-flow calculation
\cite{KanehiraEtAl2026}.  These studies establish the value of hybrid models;
they also make clear that coupling as such is not a novelty claim.

Table~\ref{tab:prior-hybrids} separates the architectures from the narrower
question addressed here.  Earlier work focuses on domain exchange,
initialisation or physical validation.  The present work instead asks whether
a late interface-and-velocity state can be handed once to a non-conforming
embedded receiver while satisfying that receiver's finite-volume constraints.

The contribution is expressed as four testable statements.  (i) Conservative
redistribution preserves every integrated panel circulation and hence the
total boundary circulation to roundoff.  (ii) A shared-face streamfunction
curl has identically zero Cartesian finite-volume divergence before receiver
sampling.  (iii) receiver-grid liquid momentum is imposed within the sparse
reconstruction rather than repaired by an unconstrained posterior shift.
(iv) Advecting $q=f c_s$ with one geometric flux per shared face makes the
global physical liquid measure telescope and preserves $0\le q\le c_s$.
The calculations below test these statements and then apply the
transition to an overturning breaker on three receiver grids.

\section{Mathematical formulation}

\subsection{Pre-contact free-surface flow}

Let $\Omega(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ be the liquid domain.  With velocity
$\boldsymbol u=\nabla\phi$, the potential satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \nabla^2\phi=0\quad\text{in }\Omega(t),\qquad
 \partial_n\phi=0\quad\text{on the bed }\Gamma_b.
 \label{eq:laplace}
\end{equation}
The periodic free surface is
$\boldsymbol X(\alpha,t)=(x(\alpha,t),z(\alpha,t))$ and carries the trace
$\psi=\phi|_{\Gamma_f}$.  With normal velocity $V_n=\partial_n\phi$,
tangential fluid velocity $V_t=\partial_s\psi$ and freely chosen marker
velocity $W_t$, the surface equations are
\begin{align}
 \boldsymbol X_t &= V_n\boldsymbol n+W_t\boldsymbol t,\\
 \psi_t &= \tfrac12V_n^2-\tfrac12V_t^2+W_tV_t-gz.
 \label{eq:surface-evolution}
\end{align}

For prescribed $\psi_f$ and zero bed flux, Green's identity gives the mixed
system
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}S_{:f}&-(\tfrac12I+K)_{:b}\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}q_f\\ \phi_b\end{bmatrix}
=(\tfrac12I+K)_{:f}\psi_f,
\label{eq:mixed-bie}
\end{equation}
where $q_f=\partial_n\phi|_{\Gamma_f}$ and $S$ and $K$ are the periodic
single- and double-layer operators.  Their Green function is
\begin{equation}
G_L(\Delta x,\Delta z)=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\log\!\left[
2\left(\cosh\frac{2\pi\Delta z}{L}-
\cos\frac{2\pi\Delta x}{L}\right)\right].
\end{equation}
Kress product weights integrate the logarithmic self interaction.  The
incident-wave normal velocity is projected onto the actual bathymetry by
subtracting its mean boundary flux and solving the compatible Neumann problem
in a zero-mean gauge.

The ordinary self rule becomes under-resolved when two distant arclength
segments approach.  Define
\begin{equation}
d_{\rm nl}=\min_{d_s(i,j)>8\overline{\Delta s}}
\|\boldsymbol X_i-\boldsymbol X_j\|,
\qquad
\overline{\Delta s}=\frac{2\pi}{N}\overline{|\boldsymbol X_\alpha|}.
\end{equation}
When $d_{\rm nl}/\overline{\Delta s}<1.5$, the complete free-surface
self block is reevaluated after trigonometric interpolation to $pN$ points,
with
\begin{equation}
p=\operatorname{clip}_{[2,16]}
\left\lceil4\overline{\Delta s}/d_{\rm nl}\right\rceil.
\end{equation}
The density and geometry are oversampled together so that the Kress splitting
remains consistent.

\subsection{Adaptive circulation representation}

The surface potential trace is equivalently represented by sheet strength
$\gamma=-\partial_s\psi$.  For a panel $j$ the integrated circulation
\begin{equation}
 \Gamma_j=-\int_{s_j}^{s_{j+1}}\gamma\,\dd s
          =\psi_{j+1}-\psi_j
 \label{eq:panel-circulation}
\end{equation}
is used as the conservative carrier during marker redistribution.  New panel
endpoints are chosen from a smoothed monitor depending on curvature, sheet
strength and inverse nonlocal gap; the cumulative circulation is interpolated
monotonically and then differenced on the new partition.  This preserves total
circulation to roundoff.  These boundary circulations represent the
irrotational potential trace; they are not bulk material vortices.

\subsection{Conservative embedded liquid aperture}

For a Cartesian receiver cell $K_i$, define the full-cell physical liquid
aperture
\begin{equation}
q_i=\frac{|K_i\cap\Omega_\ell\cap\Omega_f|}{|K_i|},
\qquad 0\le q_i\le c_{s,i},
\label{eq:q-definition}
\end{equation}
where $c_s$ is the open-fluid volume fraction and $f=q/c_s$ is conditional on
the open part of the cell.  This full-cell measure is consistent with
solid-aware VOF/embedded-boundary formulations and conservative contact-line
treatments \cite{TavaresEtAl2024,ChenEtAl2025,HuangEtAl2025,HuangEtAl2026}.
One oriented swept volume is assigned to every
shared face,
\begin{equation}
F_e=|S_e(\Delta t)\cap\Omega_\ell\cap\Omega_f|,
\qquad
q_i^{n+1}=q_i^n-|K_i|^{-1}
\sum_{e\subset\partial K_i}\operatorname{sgn}(i,e)F_e.
\label{eq:q-flux}
\end{equation}
Because the same $F_e$ enters adjacent cells with opposite signs, global
liquid volume telescopes exactly.  A pure donor satisfies
$F_{\ell,e}=0$ for $f_d=0$ and $F_{\ell,e}=F_{f,e}$ for $f_d=1$; only genuinely
mixed cut cells require a capacity restriction.  The restriction is applied
to mixed cells and their face-connected one-cell halo,
\begin{equation}
\Delta t\le0.45\min_{i\in\mathcal I}
\frac{c_{s,i}\Delta_i}
{\max_{e\subset\partial K_i}|u_e f_{s,e}|}.
\label{eq:cut-cell-step}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Receiving two-phase model and discretisation}

After the handoff the BIE is no longer advanced and no feedback is returned to
it.  In the open part of the embedded domain, the receiver solves the
dimensionless variable-density incompressible equations
\begin{align}
 \nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol u &=0,\label{eq:receiver-divergence}\\
 \partial_t(\rho\boldsymbol u)+
 \nabla\!\cdot(\rho\boldsymbol u\otimes\boldsymbol u)
 &= -\nabla p+\nabla\!\cdot(2\mu\boldsymbol D)
 +\rho\boldsymbol g+\sigma\kappa\delta_\Gamma\boldsymbol n,
 \label{eq:receiver-momentum}\\
 \partial_t f+\nabla\!\cdot(f\boldsymbol u)&=0,
 \qquad \boldsymbol D=\tfrac12
 (\nabla\boldsymbol u+\nabla\boldsymbol u^{\mathsf T}).
 \label{eq:receiver-phase}
\end{align}
Here $f$ is conditional on open fluid, while equation~\eqref{eq:q-flux}
advects the physical aperture $q=f c_s$.  Arithmetic mixture laws are used,
\begin{equation}
 \rho(f)=f\rho_\ell+(1-f)\rho_g,
 \qquad \mu(f)=f\mu_\ell+(1-f)\mu_g.
 \label{eq:mixture-laws}
\end{equation}
Length is scaled by the offshore depth $h_0$, velocity by
$\sqrt{g h_0}$ and time by $\sqrt{h_0/g}$.  Thus $Fr=1$, and the reported runs
use
\begin{equation}
 \rho_\ell=1,\quad \rho_g/\rho_\ell=1/850,\quad
 Re=40000,\quad \mu_g/\mu_\ell=
 \frac{17.4\times10^{-6}}{8.9\times10^{-4}},\quad
 Bo=We=1000.
 \label{eq:receiver-parameters}
\end{equation}
Surface tension is therefore present with $\sigma=1/Bo$; gravity is
$\boldsymbol g=(0,-1)$.  The implementation uses a balanced VOF surface-force
form and reduced pressure for gravity \cite{Popinet2009}.  The interface and
mixture properties follow the geometrical VOF formulation
\cite{HirtNichols1981,Popinet2009}.

The receiver is the cell-centred adaptive projection method of
\citet{Popinet2003}, with a Bell--Colella--Glaz predictor
\cite{BellColellaGlaz1989}, an implicit viscous solve and a multilevel pressure
Poisson solve.  The two phase momenta
$f\rho_\ell\boldsymbol u$ and $(1-f)\rho_g\boldsymbol u$ are carried with the
same geometrical phase flux and recombined after advection.  The pressure
projection tolerance is $10^{-8}$.  The accepted step is limited by the
ordinary advective CFL, the explicit capillary condition and
equation~\eqref{eq:cut-cell-step}.

The horizontal direction is periodic.  The embedded bathymetry is
impermeable and free slip ($u_n=0$, $\partial_n u_t=0$); the distant top of the
$64h_0$-high computational box uses the corresponding homogeneous symmetry
condition.  Pressure is not transferred: it is initialised to zero and the
first receiver projection constructs the dynamic pressure compatible with the
mapped velocity.  After every accepted step, wavelet adaptation uses absolute
tolerances $2\times10^{-4}$ for $q$, $2\times10^{-3}$ for each velocity
component and $8\times10^{-3}$ for $c_s$.  Maximum/minimum level pairs are
$(8,6)$, $(9,7)$ and $(10,8)$.  The conservative $q$ restriction is applied
before momentum prolongation, retained face fluxes are reprojected after a
mesh change, and the associated momentum and energy changes are recorded.

\subsection{Divergence-conforming state transfer}

The target liquid momentum is evaluated from boundary data,
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol P^\star=\rho\int_\Omega\nabla\phi\,\dd A
=\rho\oint_{\partial\Omega}\phi\boldsymbol n\,\dd s.
\label{eq:target-momentum}
\end{equation}
Two-point Gauss quadrature supplies the BIE velocity averaged over every
receiver face.  A periodic vertex streamfunction $\chi$ then defines
\begin{equation}
u_{i-1/2,k}=\frac{\chi_{i-1/2,k+1/2}-\chi_{i-1/2,k-1/2}}{\Delta z},
\quad
w_{i,k-1/2}=-\frac{\chi_{i+1/2,k-1/2}-\chi_{i-1/2,k-1/2}}{\Delta x}.
\label{eq:mac-curl}
\end{equation}
The standard face-difference divergence of this field vanishes identically.
Compatibility projections and divergence-preserving transfer between
non-conforming or multilevel grids provide the relevant numerical context
\cite{SunWheeler2006,CervoneManservisiScardovelli2011,GorgesEtAl2022}.
The sparse fit minimises
\begin{equation}
\|W_f(C_h\chi-\bar{\boldsymbol u})\|_2^2
+\lambda_s\|S\chi-\boldsymbol u_s\|_2^2
+\lambda_b\|B\chi\|_2^2
+\lambda_P\|M\chi-\boldsymbol P^\star/\rho\|_2^2
+\lambda_e\|E\chi\|_2^2+\lambda_h\|L_h\chi\|_2^2.
\label{eq:mac-fit}
\end{equation}
Here $B\chi=0$ makes the bed a streamline, $M$ imposes momentum, $E$ suppresses
exterior kinetic energy and $L_h$ regularises unconstrained modes.  After AMR,
the face flux transported by the mesh is retained and a receiver-grid
Helmholtz correction restores compatibility; no global velocity shift is
applied.  Table~\ref{tab:fit-weights} gives the production weights.  The face
term has unit weight; the Laplacian row is scaled before squaring, so the last
entry is reported as $\sqrt{\lambda_h}$.  All coordinates and fields in this
fit are nondimensional.  The reported $\lambda$ values are per-row
squared-residual multipliers: each block is premultiplied by
$\sqrt{\lambda}$, with no division by its number of rows.  Consequently their
absolute magnitudes are not cross-block physical importance factors, and the
aggregate block contributions change with row count under refinement.  A
right diagonal column equilibration scales every unknown by the inverse
Euclidean norm of its assembled column for the iterative solve and is undone
before $\chi$ is returned; it does not alter the row weights.  One set of
weights was fixed before the three-grid runs and used without level-by-level
retuning, so the reported grid results test that fixed discrete choice rather
than a resolution-invariant hyperparameter normalisation.

\section{Verification}

The tests are grouped by the property they measure.  Analytic and manufactured
solutions test the BIE and aperture operators; algebraic residuals test the
handoff constraints; long receiver runs test conservation and boundedness.
Interface contact is assessed separately in Section~\ref{sec:breaker}.

For a flat surface Fourier mode, the Dirichlet-to-Neumann eigenvalue and the
discrete velocity agree to machine precision.  A fully nonlinear solitary
wave propagated over one reference interval has relative shape error
$1.82\times10^{-5}$ and speed error $8.40\times10^{-5}$.  On a manufactured
folded interface, the unoversampled 64-panel discretisation has a relative DNO
error of 6.36 even though its linear residual is near $10^{-13}$; order-16
oversampling reduces the error to $6.91\times10^{-2}$
(figure~\ref{fig:close-quadrature}).  Repeating the factor sweep at 128 panels
reduces the unoversampled error from 2.74 to $3.56\times10^{-2}$ at factors
8--16.  The plateau under further oversampling identifies the remaining error
as boundary-discretisation rather than linear-solver error.  This two-grid,
one-gap study verifies the trigger mechanism but is not a complete
$(N,d_{\rm nl}/h,p)$ convergence map.

For sinusoidal-interface translation, the relative $L^1(q)$ errors on levels
6--8 are 0.01378, 0.007689 and 0.003538, corresponding to observed orders 0.84

0.006154 on levels 7--9 (orders 0.90 and 1.02) while preserving volume and
bounds to roundoff.  Restricting the cut-cell timestep to the interface band
requires 211 steps at level 7, compared with 758 when every cut cell is
included.

The $q$ construction is also compared directly with independently updating
the product $f c_s$ in a one-step inclined-wall kinematic reference
(table~\ref{tab:q-ablation}).  The product update both changes the physical
liquid volume and incurs a transport error; the shared-$q$ update telescopes to
roundoff on all three grids.  This is an operator test, not a substitute for
the coupled receiver experiment below.

\section{Three-grid overturning-wave experiment}
\label{sec:breaker}

\subsection{Configuration and event definition}

The source is an active $N=576$ BIE state over a periodic $1{:}30$ beach and
return slope, transferred at nondimensional BIE time 20.303125.  Conservative
raster overlap changes volume by $2.45\times10^{-13}$ relative to the source;
reconciliation with the analytic embedded bed changes 0.0868\%.  Receiver
maximum levels 8, 9 and 10 use minimum levels 6, 7 and 8, respectively, and
adapt after every accepted physical step.  VOF and vorticity fields are sampled
every 0.02 through continuation time 6.

The receiver-quadrature fits at levels 8--10 have face-flux errors
1.5496\%, 1.5505\% and 1.5510\%, surface-velocity errors 2.7794\%, 2.7791\%
and 2.7790\%, and relative receiver-momentum errors between
$6.37\times10^{-9}$ and $7.09\times10^{-9}$.  Their weighted velocity changes
from the unconstrained receiver samples are 1.05\%, 1.19\% and 2.01\%, while
the corresponding kinetic-energy changes are 0.279\%, 0.252\% and 0.320\%.
These results quantify the price of imposing momentum on each actual adaptive
receiver quadrature rather than only on the uniform export grid.

A preliminary level-9 two-time screen compared BIE states at 20.29375 and
20.303125 through receiver time 0.30.  It used the pre-production receiver in
which $f$ and $c_s$ were updated independently and multiplied, although the
post-AMR face flux was preserved; it did not use the shared-$q$ transport of
the three-grid experiment.  The maximum profile and crest differences in that
legacy screen are 0.944\% and 1.22\%, while the later run's maximum volume
drift is $1.52\times10^{-3}$.  By contrast, the production shared-$q$ level-9
run at the same BIE time has maximum drift $6.43\times10^{-8}$ through $t=6$.
The values therefore describe different discrete transport operators, not two
estimates of one run.  The legacy screen is retained as negative provenance
only; production-scheme sensitivity to handoff time remains untested.

A gas component is classified as a resolved closure if it is disconnected
from the exterior, does not touch a one-finest-cell bed guard, has area at least
$\Delta^2$, and persists for three output frames.  Four-neighbour components
are joined across the horizontal periodic seam.  Diagnostic coarsening uses
exact block liquid-area fractions at scale factors two and three and thresholds
0.25, 0.5 and 0.75.  The accepted onset-time variation is at most 0.04.

\section{Discussion}

The experiment separates stability from physical convergence.  Transporting
$q$ with shared embedded fluxes removes the previous long-time mass loss, and
the receiver-grid correction retains divergence and momentum compatibility
after AMR.  These properties remain stable through 301 output times on all
three grids.  They do not, however, determine the topology of the free
surface: the first resolved gas component appears only on level 10 and is
transient, while the upper-surface geometry remains outside the selected
fine-grid tolerance.

The level-10 cavity may be the first resolved representation of jet closure,
or it may be a resolution-dependent branch selected by the coupled AMR and
interface reconstruction.  The current data cannot distinguish these
possibilities.  A decisive impact calculation requires at least one finer
receiver grid, time-step refinement, several earlier handoff times and an
aligned experimental or high-resolution two-phase reference.  Event time,
jet-tip position, enclosed-gas area and pressure impulse should converge
together.  Air compressibility and three-dimensionality may also become
important near closure.

The contribution is therefore the conservative and divergence-conforming
state transfer, together with a three-level observation that topology
converges more slowly than invariants.  The available ablations isolate
cell-centred versus shared-face transfer, receiver curl reconstruction,
momentum and surface constraints, harmonic regularisation, product versus
shared-$q$ transport, plus a negative two-time screen of the legacy product
receiver.  They do not yet constitute a full multi-parameter sensitivity
analysis: production-scheme handoff timing, wider weight ranges, several
nonlocal gaps, time-step refinement and a second physical scenario remain
open.  A matched end-to-end accuracy--cost comparison with a
contemporary BEM--VOF or potential-flow/particle hybrid remains necessary to
establish an efficiency advantage.  The present GPU timing concerns only the
sparse handoff reconstruction; the adaptive two-phase receiver runs on the
CPU with OpenMP.  In a fixed $t\leq0.04$ repeatability screen, four threads
reduced the level-10 wall time from 57.95 to 19.82~s (2.92-fold) relative to
one thread.  The final thresholded VOF mask was identical and the relative
mass difference was $4.56\times10^{-15}$, while the final kinetic energy
differed by $2.35\times10^{-5}$ because OpenMP reductions are not bitwise
deterministic.  On level 9, eight threads were slower than four.  These short
runs select a performance configuration; they are not grid-convergence data.

\section{Conclusions}

An adaptive Euler--BIE state can be transferred to an embedded two-phase VOF
receiver without sacrificing the receiver's discrete divergence structure.
The essential choices are to transfer shared face averages, construct the MAC
field as a streamfunction curl, impose momentum in the receiver quadrature and
transport the full-cell liquid aperture $q=f c_s$ with a shared
embedded-boundary flux.

Manufactured tests show first-order aperture convergence with roundoff mass
and bound preservation.  The L8--L10 receiver continuation remains bounded through
$t=6$, with maximum relative liquid-volume drift below
$8.57\times10^{-8}$ and small post-AMR compatibility corrections.  The
level-10 trajectory contains a resolved but transient gas component and
strong crest vorticity.  Its onset and morphology are not reproduced on the
coarser grids, and the final L9/L10 profile and crest differences exceed 5\%.

The present result supports a conservative, divergence-conforming one-way
model transition and identifies a resolution-dependent topology transition.
It does not yet
support a grid-converged prediction of plunging impact, trapped-air volume or
impact impulse.
